How the math works

  1. Convert units. If your report lists mg/L (or ppm) as calcium carbonate, divide by 17.1 to get grains per gallon.
  2. Add iron. Iron also uses up softener capacity. A common dealer rule of thumb adds about 4 gpg per 1 ppm of iron; some use 3 to 5. It's an estimate. Check your softener's spec sheet for its iron limit, too.
  3. Daily grains = adjusted hardness × people × gallons per person per day.
  4. Grains between regenerations = daily grains × days.
  5. Pick a size. Softeners are sold by nominal grain capacity (24,000, 32,000, 48,000, and so on), usually rated at the maximum salt setting. The calculator picks the smallest common size where your need is at most 75% of that rating, so the unit can run at a lower, more salt-efficient setting. About 32,000 grains per cubic foot of resin is a common industry convention. Treat both as rules of thumb, and compare with the maker's own sizing chart.

Where the 75 gallons comes from

The EPA says the average American family uses more than 300 gallons of water a day at home, and roughly 70% of that is indoors (EPA WaterSense). That's about 210 gallons indoors, or about 70 gallons each for a family of three. Softeners usually treat indoor water, so 60 to 80 gallons per person is a reasonable range. If you have a big soaking tub or lots of laundry, go higher. Outdoor irrigation usually shouldn't run through the softener.

Example

A family of 4 with 15 gpg hardness and 1 ppm iron: (15 + 4) × 4 × 75 = 5,700 grains a day. Over 7 days that's 39,900 grains, so the rule-of-thumb pick is a 64,000-grain unit (39,900 is 62% of 64,000). A 48,000-grain unit would be at 83% of its rating, so it would need its maximum salt setting or more frequent regenerations.

What the calculator doesn't cover

  • Flow rate. Bigger homes with several bathrooms running at once need a valve and tank that can keep up. Check the unit's service flow rating.
  • Salt-free conditioners. They don't remove grains, so this math doesn't apply. See salt vs salt-free.
  • Health contaminants. A softener isn't a filter for bacteria, nitrate, or lead. See well water.

Quick questions

How do I calculate what size water softener I need?

Multiply hardness in grains per gallon (plus about 4 gpg per ppm of iron) by the number of people, by gallons per person per day (about 75), and by the days between regenerations (often 7). Choose a softener rated comfortably above that total.

What size water softener for a family of 4?

It depends on hardness. At 10 gpg with no iron, a family of 4 using 75 gallons each needs about 21,000 grains per week, so a 32,000-grain unit fits by the 75% rule of thumb. At 20 gpg it's about 42,000 grains, so a 64,000-grain unit.
