Well water: test first, then pick by problem

By the Test First Water team · Updated Nov 19, 2026 · 9 min read · No hands-on testing claimed: we compare published specs, policies, and certifications.

Short answer: For well water, safety first, equipment second. Test for bacteria and nitrate (the CDC's yearly list), then for hardness, iron, manganese, and hydrogen sulfide. The best water softener for well water is a salt-based softener sized for your hardness plus iron. If iron or sulfur is high, an iron and sulfur filter goes in front of it. If bacteria shows up, you need disinfection (often UV), and certification class matters.

Step 1: get a lab test, not a guess

Private wells aren't covered by EPA public water rules. The CDC says to test at least once a year for total coliform bacteria, nitrates, total dissolved solids, and pH with a state-certified lab, and to ask your health department about local contaminants (CDC). For equipment choices, also test hardness, iron, manganese, and hydrogen sulfide. See how to test.

Good sign from one seller: SpringWell's policy says it holds well-system orders until it reviews a recent water test showing the system fits your water (SpringWell shipping and returns). Any seller that will ship well equipment without seeing a test is guessing.

Read the warranty fine print for well water

SpringWell's lifetime warranty on its salt-free FutureSoft line lists well-water conditions it doesn't cover: hydrogen sulfide, oils, copper above 1.30 mg/L, and iron or manganese (SpringWell warranty). Those are common in wells. That's one more reason to test first and pick salt-based softening with iron pretreatment when your numbers call for it.

Quick questions

What is the best water softener for well water?

A salt-based ion exchange softener sized for your hardness plus iron, with a dedicated iron and sulfur filter in front if your lab test shows high iron, manganese, or hydrogen sulfide. Salt-free conditioners don't remove hardness or iron.

Do I need UV for my well?

Only if testing shows bacteria or your health department recommends it. For contaminated water, look for NSF/ANSI 55 Class A. Class B is for water that's already disinfected.

What should I test my well for before buying a system?

The CDC's yearly list (total coliform, nitrates, total dissolved solids, pH) plus hardness, iron, manganese, and hydrogen sulfide for sizing.
